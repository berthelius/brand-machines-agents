# Applying the method

## Diagnose

Identify the brand, objective and available sources. For each layer, collect a supported statement, its evidence and missing information. Distinguish documents, observed behavior and inference. Present `bm.py diagnose` as documentation coverage: it does not measure maturity or quality.

Examine dependencies: a promise expressed by the Skin must be supported by decisions, structure and operations. Prioritize interventions by their effect on the user's problem. Do not fill the seven layers with invented content to make a report look complete.

Deliver a concise diagnosis with evidence, tensions between layers and a concrete next step. Request only missing data that changes the decision.

## Propose

Retrieve the Core and relevant principles. Identify context, constraints and alternatives. Explain which principle resolves the trade-off and what cost is accepted. Use the expression appropriate to that brand.

When principles are missing, propose their formulation using the six fields from Chapter 11 and mark their status as proposed. Brand Machines' editorial aesthetic is not inherited by default. Apply the Guardian review before delivering the proposal.

## Review

Read the [review contract](review.md). Run the relevant mechanical checks, then evaluate meanings those checks cannot determine. A lexical rule only identifies a match: a critical quotation and the brand's own promise may require different judgments.

Preserve the mechanical result when documenting a semantic exception and explain the context. Do not alter the pack or its hashes to make a piece pass. If the evidence conflicts, show the conflict.

## Minimal memory

If the user requests a record of the decision, retain brand/version, piece, judgment, principles, references, responsible person when known, and observed result. Separate the decision from its later outcome. Do not turn one campaign into a general principle or store private internal reasoning: a verifiable editorial rationale is sufficient.
