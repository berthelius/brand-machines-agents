## 1. Repair first

**Status:** `approved`. **Mechanical result:** `needs_evidence`, no lexical finding.

**Passage:** “Before replacing a jacket with a repairable zipper, explore a repair.”

**Finding:** Core → Skin; principle `useful-life`; sources P1 and P2. The decision preserves useful gear and asks the reader to explore a repair. P2 offers repair pathways but says technicians can try to fix an item. The second sentence asks the reader to check the route and terms, so the proposal does not turn that pathway into a universal promise.

**Limitation:** repairability is stipulated in the scenario. No jacket has been examined, and this review cannot establish feasibility, price or timing for a real repair.

**Suggested revision:** none within the stated scenario. Keep the qualification about service terms.

## 2. Replacement by default

**Status:** `revise`. **Mechanical result:** `needs_evidence`, no lexical finding.

**Passage:** “Replace the one that still works”.

**Finding:** Skin conflicts with the study's Core formulation; principle `useful-life`; sources P1 and P2. The scenario explicitly says the current jacket still meets the need. P1 asks readers not to buy what they do not need; P2 connects care and repair with keeping gear in use. A new season alone does not supply a need in this scenario. Familiar outdoor vocabulary does not resolve the decision conflict.

**Limitation:** this finding depends on the stipulated absence of an unmet need. It is not a prohibition on all purchases or a claim about actual Patagonia campaigns.

**Suggested revision:** “If your jacket still does what you need, keep using it. When a fault appears, explore repair before replacement.”

## 3. Absolute environmental promise

**Status:** `revise`. **Mechanical result:** `revise`, check `absolute-impact-warning`.

**Passage:** “zero impact on the planet”.

**Finding:** Skin; principle `bounded-promises`; source P3, particularly “What about all that shipping?”. The cited FAQ acknowledges environmental impacts from shipping and argues for keeping gear in use. It does not establish zero impact for a used purchase. The lexical warning locates the phrase; the contradiction with the cited evidence requires reading that evidence.

**Limitation:** no product life-cycle assessment was supplied. Neither this review nor the correction quantifies an impact reduction. The check would also flag the same phrase inside a negation or quotation, and miss differently worded absolute claims.

**Suggested revision:** “If you need a jacket, consider a suitable used one. Keep existing gear in use for longer.”

## 4. An authentic citation, an unsupported promise

**Status:** `needs_evidence`. **Mechanical result:** `needs_evidence` because of missing layers only; no claim-source or lexical finding.

**Passage:** “Every repair is free and completed within 48 hours.”

**Finding:** Skin; principle `bounded-promises`; source P4. The guarantee explicitly allows a reasonable charge for wear-and-tear repairs. That contradicts universal gratuity. It provides no 48-hour commitment. P4 is a genuine, present reference in the pack, so existence and hash checks cannot establish either promise. P3's order, return and trade-in processing times cannot be substituted for a repair deadline.

**Limitation:** a narrower, separately documented offer could have different terms. It would require evidence naming the service, eligibility, territory, price and deadline. The universal free claim already needs correction; the overall status retains the unresolved evidence requirement for the deadline.

**Suggested revision:** “Ask about repair options and confirm the applicable cost and turnaround time before sending your gear.”

## 5. Necessary replacement

**Status:** `approved`. **Mechanical result:** `needs_evidence`, no lexical finding.

**Passage:** “If your jacket cannot be restored to the use you need, compare a suitable used option and a durable new replacement.”

**Finding:** Core → Skin; principle `useful-life`; sources P1, P3 and P4. P1 asks readers to consider necessity and describes useful, durable gear; P3 offers used gear; P4 recognizes replacement as one remedy. The scenario stipulates an unmet need and inability to restore the required use after qualified assessment. Considering replacement here does not contradict the study's principle. The message asks the reader to confirm suitability, availability and terms rather than promising a specific product or service outcome.

**Limitation:** the assessment is hypothetical. No actual product is declared beyond repair, and no used or new product is certified suitable. P4 does not itself dictate the shopping sequence; that sequence is the analyst's proposal within this scenario.

**Suggested revision:** none within the stated scenario. Preserve the condition and the requirement to confirm suitability.
